Five signs the tub itself is the problem

  1. They sit on the tub edge and swing their legs over one at a time.
  2. They have started skipping baths or "just washing at the sink."
  3. A grab bar and bench helped for a while, but they still need you there.
  4. They use a walker indoors or have had a hip or knee replaced.
  5. You are planning around a wheelchair in the next year or two.

Questions families ask

Does Medicare pay for grab bars or a shower seat?

Original Medicare generally does not. Medicare treats grab bars, bath seats, raised toilet seats and nonslip flooring as comfort or convenience items. Some Medicare Advantage plans include a bathroom safety or home modification benefit, so check the plan's Evidence of Coverage.

Where should grab bars go in a senior's bathroom?

Next to the toilet, at the tub or shower entry, and on the long wall inside the tub or shower. Mount them into studs or blocking, never into drywall alone, and never use a towel bar as a grab bar.

Are suction-cup grab bars safe?

They are meant for balance only, not for holding body weight, and can release without warning. For anything someone may pull on, use a bar screwed into framing.

When is it time to replace the tub?

When the person can no longer step over the tub wall safely even with a grab bar and a bench, has stopped bathing because of fear of falling, or needs a walker or wheelchair to get into the bathroom.
